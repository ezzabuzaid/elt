# Every crawl of a read asks one record of each origin

A host such as the web source gives each of its inputs its own crawl, because each input has its own memento: an interrupted crawl continues from it. Each crawl kept the manners of each origin too: the `robots.txt` rules, the breaker, the slowdown, the run of 403 answers and the start of the last request. Thus two inputs on one origin asked it twice for its `robots.txt`, and the second input requested an origin that had blocked the first one a moment before. On 2026-10-29, context-compiler's what-is-going-on app read `https://www.anthropic.com/news`, `/research` and `/engineering` as three inputs of one stream, so each paid its own 429 (backlog items 3256 and 3505). No origin had a delay of its own: two requests to one origin waited only for a `Crawl-delay` or a slowdown. Got followed redirects inside one request, so the origin that a redirect led to was asked without its `robots.txt`, its delay or its breaker (backlog item 3504), and Got retried refusals inside one request, so the crawl had to count the retries in two copied counters.

Politeness is a property of the host, not of a crawl (_Introduction to Information Retrieval_ §20.2.3: one back queue for each host). The established crawlers keep one record for each host and route every request through it:

- Heritrix keeps one `CrawlServer` for each host and port in its `ServerCache` (`ServerCache.java`, line 77, at 65529301). The record holds the `robots.txt` and the time it was read (`CrawlServer.java`, lines 66 to 67), and `PreconditionEnforcer` reads the rules again after `robotsValidityDurationSeconds`, 24 hours (`PreconditionEnforcer.java`, line 75). Its delay is at least `minDelayMs`, 3 seconds (`DispositionProcessor.java`, lines 72 to 114). It follows no redirect itself (`FetchHTTPRequest.java`, lines 811 to 812): the `Location` is an outlink, a URI of its own.
- Nutch keeps one `FetchItemQueue` for each host (`FetchItemQueues.java`, lines 48 and 170, at e29a1d46), with `fetcher.server.delay` between two fetches, and it adds an exponential back-off to the queue's next fetch time after an exception (`FetchItemQueues.java`, line 331; `fetcher.exceptions.per.queue.delay` starts at the server delay). Its HostDb keeps each host across cycles (`HostDatum.java`). It follows no redirect by default (`http.redirect.max` 0) and asks `robots.txt` for each hop that it follows (`FetcherThread.java`, line 387).
- StormCrawler waits `fetcher.server.delay`, 1 second, between two fetches of one queue, and a positive `Crawl-delay` replaces it (`crawler-default.yaml`, line 22; `CrawlDelayPolicy.java`, lines 84 to 116, at 5452c1c0). It follows no redirect (`http.allow.redirects: false`, line 167). Its URLFrontier blocks a host that answers 429 or 503 until the `Retry-After`, at most `urlfrontier.max.retry.after`, 86400 seconds (`HostBackoff.java`, line 99; `Constants.java`, line 97), and it keeps a failed `robots.txt` lookup for an hour (`robots.error.cache.spec`, line 333).

Thus:

1. **`Origins` is the record of each origin for one read.** The host makes one when the read opens and gives it to each crawler of the read. [ADR 0007](./0007-the-read-makes-its-crawlers.md) replaces how: the read makes its crawlers, and the record is internal. A record keeps the origin's `robots.txt` rules and the time it read them, its breaker, its slowdown, its run of 403 answers, the start of its last request, and the documents of one-step expansions that the read downloaded there.
2. **The rules last 24 hours.** A record reads `robots.txt` again only when its rules are 24 hours old, the longest that RFC 9309 §2.4 allows. A request that reads them while another waits for them shares the one answer.
3. **Each origin has a delay.** Two requests to one origin start at least one second apart, as StormCrawler's `fetcher.server.delay`, or a positive `Crawl-delay` apart, at most 60 seconds. One second keeps one origin at one request a second, while a crawl of many origins still moves. The slowdown adds to the delay, as Nutch adds its back-off to the server delay.
4. **The breaker belongs to the origin.** A 429, a 503 with `Retry-After` (RFC 9110 §10.2.3), three 403 answers in a row, or a `robots.txt` that cannot be read, opens the breaker of the origin that answered, until the `Retry-After`, at most one day later, or one hour later without one. Every crawl of the read then holds that origin's URLs. A breaker that opened in a read stays open to the end of the read, as Nutch clears a blocked host's queue for the round.
5. **A redirect is a request of its own.** The crawl asks Got to follow no redirect. The `Location` of a 300, 301, 302, 303, 307 or 308 answer is a frontier entry that continues the request: it keeps its roles, its provenance, the admissions of its readers and its Got context, as StormCrawler transfers metadata to a redirect target. It passes the `robots.txt`, the delay and the breaker of its own origin. The requested URL's visit is a redirect outcome of its own, as Nutch's `STATUS_FETCH_REDIR_TEMP` and `_PERM` and StormCrawler's `REDIRECTION`: the memento keeps its target and whether it is permanent (301 or 308) in place of a body digest, and the revisit policy gets `{ redirect, permanent, change }`. A target that stays is `unmodified`, one that moves is `modified`, and the first redirect after a body is `unknown`. Every `IntervalRevisit` schedules a redirect as a visit that succeeded with that change, as Nutch's `CrawlDbReducer` gives a redirect to `setFetchSchedule` with a fetched page (`CrawlDbReducer.java`, lines 236 to 265). Nutch leaves a redirect's change unknown (NUTCH-1422), because it has no signature; the target is the redirect's content here, so it can be compared. Thus `AdaptiveRevisit` and `ChangeRateRevisit` count a moved target as a change, `FixedRevisit` and `HintRevisit` wait their interval, `OnceRevisit` never asks again, and `AlwaysRevisit` asks in every crawl. A chain stops after 10 redirects, as Got's `maxRedirects`. The routes give `redirect` as before.
6. **Got retries no refusal.** Each request of a crawl, and each request through `Origins.http`, removes the refusals, 429 and 5xx, from Got's retried statuses and keeps the network errors and the 408. A refused URL is due again by its revisit policy. The two copied refusal counters are gone.
7. **Requests outside a frontier pass the same record.** `Origins.http` is the client for requests that no crawl's frontier holds, such as the HTML companion, the advertised alternates and the browser request of an article. Its hooks ask the record of each request's origin, and of each redirect that Got follows for it, through Got's awaited `beforeRedirect` hook, as a crawl's request does, and they count each answer.
8. **The expansion budget is the origin's for each read.** ADR 0005 gives 8 documents for each run and origin; all the crawls of a read now share them.
9. **What outlives a read is the origin's memento.** `Origins.snapshot()` gives the rules with the time they were read, the end of the block and the slowdown. The host keeps it, as Nutch's HostDb. The crawl memento keeps only the frontier: its pending entries, its readers' states, its starting origins, its known URLs and its starting pages.

## Considered Options

- **Keep the manners in each crawl, and copy them from one crawl to the next.** Two crawls of a read that run at the same time still disagree, and every new piece of state needs one more copy.
- **One crawler for each read, with the revisit policy as a crawl option.** The revisit policy belongs to the copy (a full refresh uses `AlwaysRevisit`), so it would become an option of each crawl call, and the frontier and the manners would still be one object.
- **Let Got follow redirects, and check the target in its `beforeRedirect` hook.** A target that two URLs reach would download twice, and a redirect would have no entry of its own in the memento.
- **One record of each origin for each read, given to every crawler; each redirect a frontier entry.** This option was selected.

## Consequences

- A second input on an origin asks no `robots.txt` that the first read less than 24 hours ago, waits the origin's delay after the first input's last request, and sends nothing to an origin that blocked the first input.
- Every origin costs at least one second between two requests. A crawl of one website of 1000 pages takes at least 1000 seconds. Tests of the crawler take longer by the same rule.
- A page that a redirect reaches is handed back with the target as its `url`. Its `listingUrl`, `lastModified` and `published` are those of the URL that redirected. Two URLs that redirect to one target download it once in a run.
- A host that keeps no origins' memento starts each read with no records: it reads `robots.txt` again, and a block or a slowdown does not outlive the read.
- The source's own rate limit of two request starts a second is gone: the delay of each origin replaces it.
