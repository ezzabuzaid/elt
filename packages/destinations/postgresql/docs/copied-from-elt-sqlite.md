# Code copied from elt-sqlite

The SQLite destination and the Postgres destination both number their commits and give positions. The rules that make a commit a position are in `@eltjs/elt` (`position.ts`, backlog #4289): the sweep line over the loads, `parsePosition`, `positionsAfter`, `readingExpiry` and `staleness`. Each destination reads its numbering from its own store and gives it to them. The derived datasets share three rules there too (backlog #4649): `refreshComputed` decides what is due and runs the pool of rule calls, `refreshView` decides when a view waits, and `DerivedStatus` gives the states of `derived_status`. The pass status is there too (backlog #4669): its types, `PassState`, `PassStatus` and `StreamPassStatus`, and `combinedPass`, which shows the latest passes of several connections as one pass. The declaration of a table target is there too, since elt-sqlserver made it a third place: `Identifiers.reader` gives the name of a reader view and refuses one that the sync history publishes, `targetFileReads` checks the columns that a host declares (one field each, one key at most, a file in a column of its kind), `schemaColumns` gives the columns that a destination infers from the JSON Schema of a stream, and `requireDescribed` refuses an explicit column of a field that the stream does not describe. This package keeps its own copy of the other code below, and does not share it with elt-sqlite yet.

The reason is the Rule of Three. It counts places, not packages. Code that a second place needs is copied, and it is extracted only when a third place needs it. Ezz moved the extraction before the third destination (ADR 0012 of elt-sqlserver), so the shared parts move to `@eltjs/elt` from these two copies. Until then, a fix in one copy is made in the other copy too, and the commit names both files.

Paths on the left are in `packages/destinations/sqlite/src`. Paths on the right are in `packages/destinations/postgresql/src`.

## Knowledge in both, written apart

- The external schema: a reader sees the columns of a stream and `loaded_at`, and never `committed_in`, `_elt_row_hash` or `replaced_in`. SQLite keeps it in `shown` of `sqlite-writer.ts` (the reader view) and in `selectAsOf` of `sqlite-as-of.ts` (each view of a reading, `asOfSelect` and `asOfDefinitions`), which leaves out `internalColumns` of `sqlite-identifiers.ts`. Postgres keeps it in the columns of `TargetReaderView` that `postgres-writer.ts` gives, which are also its view of a position. A field cannot take one of these names: the `ownColumns` list of `sqlite-identifiers.ts` reserves them all. The list of `postgres-identifiers.ts` reserves `loaded_at`, `committed_in` and `replaced_in`, and its system columns; the Postgres naming rule already turns a field `_elt_row_hash` into `elt_row_hash`.

- A load of a connector that is still open when a new load of the connector opens is closed by the new load. A closed load cannot commit. SQLite checks `closed_at` in the `INSERT` of the commit, because it runs one write transaction at a time. Postgres locks the row of the load `FOR SHARE`.
- A clear is a load of one commit, which does not close the open loads of its connector.
